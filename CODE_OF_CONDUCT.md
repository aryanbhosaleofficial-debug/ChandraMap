# Code of Conduct

This Code of Conduct defines expected behavior for everyone participating in
the ChandraMap community — as a contributor, maintainer, reviewer, or
participant in project discussions. It exists to keep collaboration
respectful, inclusive, and professional while preserving the rigorous
technical and research debate this project depends on.

## Our Pledge

We, as members, contributors, and maintainers of ChandraMap, pledge to make
participation in our community a respectful and harassment-free experience
for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability,
ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity or expression, level of
experience, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal
appearance, race, caste, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming,
diverse, and healthy community, and to a serious open-source and research
project that people from many backgrounds and experience levels can
contribute to productively.

Technical and research disagreement is a normal and healthy part of
engineering and scientific work. Disagreement about architecture, code,
benchmarks, research methods, models, algorithms, metrics, or experimental
results is expected and welcome. What matters is that disagreement stays
respectful and focused on the work rather than the person.

## Our Standards

### Examples of Positive Behavior

- Using respectful and inclusive language.
- Respecting differing technical viewpoints, experience levels, and
  approaches.
- Giving constructive, specific technical feedback.
- Accepting constructive criticism professionally.
- Focusing criticism on ideas, code, experiments, or results rather than on
  individuals.
- Acknowledging mistakes and correcting them.
- Giving credit to contributors and respecting research attribution.
- Communicating clearly and patiently during disagreements.
- Showing consideration for contributors across different backgrounds,
  experience levels, and time zones.
- Respecting maintainers' decisions while using appropriate channels to
  raise concerns.

### Examples of Unacceptable Behavior

- Harassment, in any form.
- Discriminatory remarks or jokes.
- Personal attacks, insults, or targeted humiliation.
- Threats or intimidation.
- Stalking or repeated unwanted contact.
- Sexualized language or imagery in project spaces.
- Trolling or deliberate disruption of discussions.
- Doxxing or publishing another person's private information without their
  explicit permission.
- Sustained hostile behavior toward another participant.
- Knowingly making false accusations intended to harm another contributor.
- Retaliation against someone who reports misconduct in good faith or
  participates in an investigation.
- Abusing project communication channels (issues, pull requests,
  discussions) for purposes unrelated to their intent.
- Deliberately derailing technical discussions.
- Plagiarism or intentional misrepresentation of another contributor's work
  when used to harass or misrepresent authorship.
- Attempting to pressure contributors through threats or harassment.

Normal technical disagreement is not, by itself, unacceptable behavior.
Strong, direct technical criticism is acceptable when it is grounded in
evidence, implementation details, methodology, reproducibility, architecture,
or benchmark validity, and expressed professionally. For example:

> "The experiment does not yet support this conclusion because the
> evaluation uses the same points used to fit the transform."

is acceptable rigorous review, while:

> "You clearly don't understand research."

is not. Likewise, "This implementation introduces a regression on the
scale-stress benchmark" is acceptable; "Your code is garbage" is not. This
Code of Conduct exists to distinguish rigorous, evidence-based review from
personal hostility — not to discourage the former.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces, and also applies
when an individual is officially representing the project in public spaces.
Project spaces include, but are not limited to:

- Repository Issues and Pull Requests
- Code reviews
- GitHub Discussions, if enabled
- Project documentation
- Research, benchmark, and technical discussions related to the project
- Project-related communication channels
- Community events or meetings associated with ChandraMap

A person may be considered to represent the project when using an official
project account, acting as a maintainer, speaking on behalf of ChandraMap,
or representing the project at an event. This Code of Conduct does not
extend to unrelated personal conduct outside these contexts.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, or have any other
concerns, please report it through the private contact method documented by
the project maintainers, where one is published. If no dedicated private
reporting channel is currently published, please avoid posting sensitive
personal details publicly, and contact a repository maintainer directly
through an available channel (for example, by opening a private
communication with a maintainer listed on the repository) rather than
through a public issue.

A report is most useful, where relevant and available, if it includes:

- A description of what happened.
- The approximate date and time.
- The location or context of the incident (e.g. a specific issue, PR, or
  discussion thread).
- Links to relevant public interactions.
- The people involved, and any witnesses, if applicable.
- Supporting screenshots or records, if available.

You are not required to gather extensive evidence before reporting, and you
should not include more sensitive personal information than is necessary to
describe the incident.

Community misconduct (harassment, threats, discriminatory remarks,
retaliation, personal attacks, and similar behavior) should be reported
through this Code of Conduct's reporting process. Security vulnerabilities
(such as a code vulnerability, leaked credentials, an unsafe dependency, an
authentication issue, or a code execution issue) are a separate category and
should instead be reported according to [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md).

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these
standards of acceptable behavior. This includes:

- Responding to reports in a timely and fair manner.
- Reviewing available evidence.
- Protecting the privacy of reporters and affected parties where possible.
- Applying consequences that are proportional to the behavior.
- Removing, editing, or rejecting content that violates this Code of
  Conduct.
- Limiting participation when necessary to protect the community.
- Documenting enforcement decisions internally where appropriate.
- Avoiding conflicts of interest.

If a maintainer is directly involved in a reported incident, they should not
be the sole person deciding the outcome where another suitable maintainer or
reviewer is available to help evaluate the report.

Enforcement applies equally regardless of contributor status. Maintainers,
frequent contributors, first-time contributors, researchers, and external
collaborators are all held to the same standards. Valuable technical
contributions do not excuse misconduct.

## Enforcement Guidelines

Maintainers will follow these guidelines in determining the consequences for
any action they deem to violate this Code of Conduct. Consequences are
proportional to the severity and context of the behavior; the following
levels are a general framework, not an automatic or strictly sequential
process.

### 1. Correction

**Community Impact:** Use of inappropriate language or other behavior
deemed unprofessional or unwelcome, without a broader pattern.

**Consequence:** A private, clarifying conversation with the individual
involved, explaining the concern and requesting a change in behavior.
Inappropriate content may be removed or edited.

### 2. Warning

**Community Impact:** A more serious incident, or a pattern of repeated
inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence:** A formal warning with clear expectations for future
behavior, and notice that continued behavior of this kind may lead to
further restrictions.

### 3. Temporary Restriction

**Community Impact:** A serious violation of community standards, including
sustained inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence:** A temporary restriction from interacting with the project
— for example, from commenting, opening issues or pull requests, or
otherwise participating — for a period determined by the maintainers based
on the severity of the incident.

### 4. Permanent Restriction

**Community Impact:** Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community
standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment of an
individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of groups of individuals.

**Consequence:** A permanent restriction from participating in project
spaces, or other repository-level restrictions available to the
maintainers, when continued participation would create unacceptable risk to
the community.

These consequences are not automatic. Maintainers will consider context,
severity, intent, and history before determining an appropriate response.

## Content Moderation

Maintainers may edit, hide, remove, or reject comments, commits, code,
documentation, issues, pull requests, or other contributions that are not
aligned with this Code of Conduct, and may reject contributions associated
with abusive behavior when necessary to protect the community.

This authority is intended for enforcing behavioral standards, not for
suppressing legitimate technical criticism. This Code of Conduct must not
be used to silence respectful disagreement, research criticism, code
review, benchmark scrutiny, or discussion of technical errors — but
"technical disagreement" is never an excuse for harassment, insults, or
intimidation.

Where relevant to community behavior specifically, unacceptable conduct can
also include deliberately taking credit for another contributor's work,
targeted plagiarism used to misrepresent authorship, harassment carried out
under the guise of research disagreement, or intentionally falsifying
accusations against another contributor. Detailed scientific and benchmark
integrity requirements belong in [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md) and
project research documentation, not in this Code of Conduct.

## Appeals

Where practical, a person affected by an enforcement decision may request a
review by another available maintainer who was not directly involved in the
original incident.

## Privacy and Confidentiality

Maintainers will handle reports with appropriate discretion. Information
related to a report will be shared only to the extent reasonably necessary
to evaluate and respond to it. Complete confidentiality cannot always be
guaranteed — for example, some information may need to be shared with other
maintainers to fairly assess or act on a report — but reporters' and
affected parties' privacy and safety will be respected throughout the
process.

## No Retaliation

Retaliation against someone for reporting misconduct in good faith,
participating in an investigation, providing relevant information, or
supporting someone affected by misconduct is not acceptable and will itself
be treated as a Code of Conduct violation.

At the same time, knowingly making an intentionally false report is also
considered misconduct. This should not discourage good-faith reports made
under uncertainty — reporters are not expected to have complete certainty
about every fact before reporting.
